## 2. Business and economics
**Segments.** (a) Aerospace Products: CFM56 / V2500 module refurbishment and exchange, engine sales, PMA parts, MRE contract (maintenance) revenue; (b) Aviation Leasing, shifting to asset-light via Strategic Capital Initiative (SCI) partnership SPVs; (c) new FTAI Power (CFM56-based 25MW aeroderivative turbines, "Mod-1").

**Reported numbers.**
- FY2025: Adj. EBITDA ~$1.19B ($1.2B headline; Aerospace $671.3M, Leasing ~$609M), net income attributable $477.5M, diluted EPS $4.60, aerospace products revenue $1.6B (Q4 release, https://finviz.com/news/322479/...). ## 3. The obvious narrative (baseline to beat)
CFM56 is the most-flown narrowbody engine; Airbus/Boeing backlogs are "sold out for years," LEAP durability keeps legacy fleets flying, so shop visits stay elevated into the 2030s. FTAI's vertically integrated Module Factory + PMA + 25% share ambition = 40% compound growth, EBITDA $1.05B (2026) -> $2.3B (2027). Asset-light SCI converts leasing into fee income and recycles capital. Power adds a free option: $1.465B hyperscaler PO on cheap CFM56 core supply. Sell-side: Strong Buy, PTs $290-$600 (stockanalysis); short report is "resolved" (Audit Committee found allegations without merit, Feb 2025, https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/ftai-aviation-audit-committee-completes-independent-review). Counter-points a first-pass GenAI answer would also list: leverage, customer concentration, short-seller history.

## 4. Live debates and what could settle them
1. **Quality of earnings / true FCF.** Muddy Waters alleged ~80% of Aerospace EBITDA derived from gains on sale (mostly engine sales) and that one engine sale was counted as three module sales (https://www.stockopedia.com/... summary of report; see Muddy Waters site [UNVERIFIED link]). Company: unfounded. Settle with: segment disclosures in 10-K/10-Q (engine/module sales vs. MRE revenue), inventory days, receivables, SEC XBRL cash flow lines, and the exact adj. FCF reconciliation.
2. **Inventory build vs. sell-through.** $1.54B inventory vs. 1H26 aerospace products revenue $1.21B. Settle with: inventory/COGS turns by quarter, 10-Q inventory footnote (engines/modules/parts/WIP), lease-to-inventory transfers.
3. **Margin trend.** 40% (Q4-25 35%) -> 29% as workscope mix shifts; is this mix or competitive pricing? Settle with: $/module and EBITDA/module history, Safran spare-parts pricing commentary, Q3 margin.
4. **CFM56 shop-visit durability / peak.** Safran H1-26: civil aftermarket parts +27.9% in USD, CFM56 benefited from favorable workscope mix; LEAP deliveries 1,030 in H1 (+41%) (https://www.safran-group.com/pressroom/safran-reports-its-first-half-2026-results-2026-07-28). Settle with: FAA registry CFM56 fleet by age, airline retirements, Cirium/ch-aviation fleet counts, 737NG/A320ceo parking data.
5. **Market share / module exchange vs. OEM and other MROs.** 25% share claim; denominator rarely disclosed. Settle by estimating global CFM56 shop visits (installed base x visit interval) vs. 1,200 modules/yr.
6. **PMA adoption.** FAA PMA database lists approved parts; compare count with revenue disclosed.
7. **SCI economics.** Leasing EBITDA cut $100M for 2026 as sale timing slipped; fees $7M/quarter vs. $475M leasing guide; gains declining ($2.5M vs $34.6M). What recurring fees at scale? Settle with: 10-Q related-party notes, SPV size, fee rates.
8. **Power: real or hype.** One customer (hyperscaler, undisclosed), JV with Chinese-owned Jereh [Jereh ownership UNVERIFIED], first unit not yet delivered, 2027 EBITDA $450M = 20% of guide. Settle with: unit ASP (PO $1.465B / units; units not disclosed), prepayment schedule, EPA/state air permits for data-center sites, GE LM2500/ Mitsubishi / Solar Turbines comparison pricing.

## 5. Candidate variant hypotheses
**H1 (Short / negative skew): "EBITDA is not cash." GAAP CFO of -$311M (2025) and -$265M (1H26) with inventory $1.54B means growth is financed by debt and SCI sale proceeds; if SCI/engine-sale proceeds slow, the stock is priced for ~21x EBITDA on non-cash earnings.** Meaningful: $3.45B debt on $404M equity; 2026 FCF already cut $37M; each $350M of semiannual inventory build equals ~1/3 of annual EBITDA. Tests: XBRL cash-flow bridge, inventory turns, adj. FCF reconciliation, 10-Q inventory footnote. Evidence for: negative CFO two consecutive years, rising inventory (see table), Leasing guide cut. Against: inventory may be core engines for Power and for sale (prepayments), asset-sale proceeds are real cash; company claims 60-70% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion; Muddy Waters allegations were cleared by audit committee. Needs peer comparison (AAR, HEICO, Willis Lease CFO conversion).

**H2 (Short, narrower): Module margin compression and share-gain "buying".** Margin fell ~500bp y/y while volume +61%; if per-module EBITDA ($ and %) is flat-to-down with rising inventory per module, consensus 2027 $1.4B Aerospace is aggressive. Tests: modules x $/module trend (limited disclosure), Safran pricing, PMA approvals. Evidence: management itself cites 25% vs 40% margin work mix. Against: absolute profit per module up (their claim).

**H3 (Long): Power is mispriced as an option when it is largely contracted.** $1.465B PO with prepayments vs. $450M 2027 EBITDA guide described as conservative (<100 units). If units shipped reach 100 at disclosed pricing, upside to the $450-750M range. Tests: implied ASP, hyperscaler identity (public filings of hyperscaler power purchases), EIA-860/interconnection queues, air permits. Risk: no delivered unit yet; Chinese JV partner supply chain and tariff/sanctions [UNVERIFIED]; AI capex sentiment (Sept sell-off).

**H4 (Pair/neutral): CFM56 peak shop-visit timing.** Retirement curve vs. LEAP durability; test with FAA registry + airline fleet tables. Less differentiated: well covered by sell-side.

## 7. Modelability

Quarterly XBRL back to ~2013. Drivers: modules produced (296 Q2-26; 1,200 FY26 guide), segment EBITDA/module (derivable: ~$0.84M Q2 on all-in basis, not module-only; rough), margin by workscope mix, MRE revenue, inventory turns, leasing fleet and SCI AUM x fee, power units x ASP x margin. Gaps: no disclosed module ASP or external vs. internal module split; no unit count in Power PO; adj. FCF reconciliation; engine-sales vs. MRO revenue split (the Muddy Waters point); segment cash flows. A three-statement model is feasible but segment revenue/margin driver precision is limited; best used as scenario bridge to 2027 guide.
