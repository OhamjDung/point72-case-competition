## 3. The obvious narrative

Consensus and a first-pass GenAI answer would say: DKS is the best-in-class sporting goods operator with a long runway from House of Sport, GameChanger and Media Network; Foot Locker is a "clean out the garage" turnaround with $100-125M of cost synergies (deal announcement, https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/features/mergers-and-acquisitions/dicks-sporting-goods-to-acquire-foot-locker-in-2-5-billion-deal ); the August 25 sell-off is an overreaction to a footwear air pocket; at ~11x forward earnings and a Buy consensus (mean PT $158, 17% upside) it is a quality compounder on sale. The bear version: Nike dependence (31% of purchases in FY25, 25% in FY24 per the 10-Ks), a promotional footwear market, tariffs, and a bad acquisition.

What that narrative skips (see sections 4-5): the deal was sold as EPS-accretive in the first full year, but FY26 guide EPS of $11-12 is about 20% below DKS-only FY25 EPS of $14.58; and the DICK'S Business margin print flattered by tariff refunds.

## 4. The live debates and the public data that can settle them

1. **Is the Foot Locker guide cut a one-time reset or the first of several?** Data: Foot Locker inventory (derived ~$1.96B vs $1.709B at 2025-08-02 under old ownership; sales down about 6%); Q3 and Q4 gross margin (Q4 FY25 was 25.0%, Q2 FY26 25.7%); store-closure and inventory-charge cadence (10-Q); Nike and peer inventory and markdown commentary; Q3 print on 2026-11-24.
2. **Is the DICK'S Business growth durable or pulled forward by the World Cup and tariff-price inflation?** The guide implies H2 comps of about -0.2% to +2.7% against H1 +5.4% (my calc, h2_implied_by_guidance.csv). Data: Census NAICS 45111 monthly sales, BLS footwear CPI, Placer.ai visits, Q3 comp.
3. **Is DICK'S gaining or losing share?** Management says it gained share; the Census sporting-goods-store proxy says otherwise for Q2 (below).
4. **Is the cash model strained?** FY25 operating cash flow $1.54B vs gross capex $1.14B, buybacks $0.35B and dividends ~$0.4B (XBRL https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001089063.json); FY26 gross capex guide $1.6B ($1.4B net) vs H1 operating cash flow $792M and cash down $440M in H1. The $1.0B notes at 6.2-6.9% add about $66M a year of interest (about $0.53 per share after tax, my calc). Data: Q3 cash flow, debt use of proceeds, buyback pace.
5. **Do House of Sport stores earn their capital, and do they cannibalize?** 2024 Placer.ai article put House of Sport at about $35M sales, 20% EBITDA margin, 35% cash-on-cash on $18.5M (https://www.placer.ai/blog/dicks-sporting-goods-new-store-formats-driving-visit-outperformance, dated 2024-03-15). No public disclosure of current store-level economics or cannibalization. Dead-end-ish; see trail.
6. **Is the Nike relationship a tailwind or a risk?** Nike Q1 FY27 (quarter to 2026-08-31): NA wholesale +9%, Nike Direct NA -6%; Nike guides FY27 revenue down high single digits [UNVERIFIED, secondary] (verified against the exhibit text: NA wholesale $2,981M vs $2,736M, NA Direct $2,146M vs $2,284M, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000184/q1fy27exhibit991er.htm; the high-single-digit revenue guide is from a secondary summary [UNVERIFIED]).
7. **Goodwill and intangibles.** Foot Locker goodwill $591M and intangible assets about $763M total balance sheet (10-Q) against a business now guided to a loss. No impairment disclosed through Q2.

## 5. Variant hypotheses

### H1 (short-lean, lower confidence): DICK'S core margin and growth are flattered by timing, and the guide implies a sharp H2 slowdown

**Claim.** The DICK'S Business Q2 print overstates run-rate. (a) Q2 merchandise margin was up 137 bps "due primarily to IEEPA tariff refunds attributable to tariff costs incurred in the current year" (10-Q MD&A). The Q2 release says $59.0M of refunds were received, $38.1M tied to prior-year costs (excluded from non-GAAP, booked in corporate) and so about $20.9M (my subtraction) tied to current-year costs and left in the segment. That is roughly 54 bps of Q2 segment sales. I do NOT headline a "tariff-neutral" margin: the underlying tariff cost was probably expensed in Q1 or Q2, so on an H1 basis the effect is closer to a timing shift, and I cannot compute the exact neutral margin without knowing when the cost hit COGS or how the refund splits across segments. The defensible statement is: reported DICK'S segment margin fell 54 bps in H1 (11.71% vs 12.25%) and 42 bps in Q2 (12.6% vs 13.0%), and Q2 is flattered by refund timing; the refunds are now "substantially all" received, so they do not recur. (b) The guide implies a sharp H2 slowdown, and this is the cleanest settleable test. DICK'S FY26 comp guide of +2.5% to +4.0% with H1 at +5.4% implies H2 comps of about -0.2% to +2.7% (h2_implied_by_guidance.csv). On a two-year stack, H2 FY25 comps were Q3 +5.7% and Q4 +3.1% (about +4.4%), so the implied H2 FY26 stack is about 4.2 to 7.1 points versus 10.1 points in H1 (Q1 6.0+4.5, Q2 4.9+5.0). Either management is sandbagging or demand is genuinely slowing once the World Cup and refunds fade; Q3 on 2026-11-24 resolves it. Implied H2 segment margin is 9.5-10.1% (h2_implied_by_guidance.csv). (c) Weak supporting leg: Census sporting goods store sales (NAICS 45111) rose +10.3% year on year for May-July 2026 (July preliminary) versus DKS Business sales +5.6%, and DKS's share of that proxy was 22.7% (2024), 24.0% (2025), 23.0% (2026) (census_marts_45111_sporting_goods_stores.csv). I checked for a Foot Locker reclassification artifact: Census shoe stores (NAICS 4482) May-Jul were $8.87B in 2026 vs $8.81B in 2025 (+0.7%), so Foot Locker did not visibly move out of shoe stores into 45111, and the 45111 acceleration was gradual (Jan +3.5%, Feb +8.1%, Mar +4.6%, Apr +11.0%, May +10.1%, Jun +13.4%, Jul +7.4%) rather than a step. The proxy also includes peers and non-DKS channels, so it is directional only.

**Why it matters.** Core segment profit of ~$1.57B is the whole equity story; each 100 bps of DICK'S segment margin on $14.6B is about $146M (about $1.2 per share after tax at 29% on 89M shares), more than the entire Foot Locker guide range.

**Test datasets.** Q3 comp and segment margin on 2026-11-24 (primary test); Census MARTS (monthly, revised); company 10-Q segment tables; BLS CPI; Placer.ai visit trends.

**For.** Refund timing above; H2 implied stack falling from 10.1 to 4-7 points; SG&A deleverage of 96 bps in Q2 (10-Q); Placer.ai showed DICK'S banner visits -3.1% YoY in Q1 2026 (secondary snippet, https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/traffic-softens-but-growth-levers-remain-for-dicks-gap-and-lululemon [UNVERIFIED date and exact figure]); ASO Q2 comps -0.4% with footwear sales -1.0% (https://academy.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/academy-sports-outdoors-reports-second-quarter-fiscal-2026 via search snippet) and JD Sports reporting a "tough" footwear market (https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/jd-sports-on-track-to-deliver-full-year-ebit-guidance/10019.html [UNVERIFIED period]).

**Against.** Census July is preliminary; category membership differs (ASO, Hibbett, Big 5, others may be taking share, and the Census sample is not DKS-specific); the 2025 comparison (-1.0% industry vs +5% DKS comps) shows the proxy is noisy; fiscal months do not align exactly; I cannot split refunds between segments or time their COGS impact; management said Q3 is where margin pressure peaks, so the guide may be deliberately conservative; insiders bought about $5.6M after the cut.

### H2 (short or event-driven, best supported): the Foot Locker H2 guide looks fragile because inventory is growing while sales shrink

**Claim.** Foot Locker inventory is growing while its sales shrink. Derived from disclosed figures (the company rounds the segment to $0.1B): at 2026-08-01, total inventory $5,565M less DICK'S inventory of about $3,608M (the Q2 release says DICK'S inventory is +6% on $3,404M at 2025-08-02) gives about $1.96B for Foot Locker; at 2026-01-31, $4,908M less about $3,383M (DICK'S $3,350M at 2025-02-01, +1% per the Q4 release) gives about $1.53B (company says $1.5B). Under old ownership Foot Locker went from $1,648M (2025-02-01) to $1,709M (2025-08-02) (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/850209/000143774925028146/floc20250625_10q.htm). So over the same February-to-August window, Foot Locker's inventory build was about +$430M (+28%) this year against +$61M (+3.7%) last year, and year on year it is about +$250M (+14-15%) with Q2 sales down about 6% ($1,737M vs $1,851M). Caution: the January 2026 base is post a $218M liquidation and a store-closure program, which inflates the percent, so the dollar build is the better measure, and part could be deliberate stock for Fast Break or holiday. Gross margin is 25.7% against 27.1% for Foot Locker in Q2 FY25 and 28.9% for FY24. Management expects promotions to continue "through at least Q4" and the implied H2 segment loss is -$66M to -$26M. Two-year stack: Foot Locker's implied H2 FY26 proforma comp is about -2.4% to +1.4% (FY guide -2.0% to 0.0% with H1 at -1.6%); H2 FY25 comps were Q3 -4.7% and Q4 -3.4% (about -4.0%), so the implied H2 two-year stack is about -6.4% to -2.6%, against -5.8% in Q2 (-3.6% plus -2.2%) and -2.3% in Q1. The low end of the guide therefore assumes only a Q2-like run rate, while the high end needs a real improvement.

**Why it matters.** 100 bps of Foot Locker gross margin on about $4.0B of H2 sales is about $40M, which is the entire width of the segment guide range. Total charges reaching $750M, goodwill of $591M and intangibles of ~$710M (at acquisition) against a $2.5B purchase price put impairment on the table if H2 misses. Inventory step-ups and write-downs are the usual path.

**Test datasets.** DKS segment inventory (disclosed only to the nearest $0.1B; derive as above), Q3 gross margin by segment on 2026-11-24, store-closure counts (store locator), Nike/adidas/On/Deckers inventory commentary, BLS footwear CPI, Google Trends.

**For.** Three of four Foot Locker quarters at a loss (Q3 FY25 -$46M, Q4 -$6M, Q1 +$17M, Q2 -$32M) (footlocker_segment_trajectory.csv); guidance already cut twice in direction; proforma Foot Locker comps were -3.3% for FY25 and -1.6% for H1 FY26.

**Against.** The company deliberately built a conservative guide this time (cut by $190M at the midpoint of segment profit, per fl_guidance_evolution.csv); Fast Break stores (250+) "definitely outperformed" legacy stores per management on the call (https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/09/01/dicks-sporting-goods-dks-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/ , summarised by fetch tool); Nike NA wholesale +9% suggests product supply exists; DICK'S says the $100-125M cost synergies are intact "over the medium term"; part of the inventory build may be deliberate (Fast Break, Salomon launch, holiday pre-buys), and the guide's low end only requires the Q2 run rate to persist.

### H3 (long-lean, valuation): the market is paying roughly nothing for Foot Locker and about 10-11x earnings for the core

**Claim.** If Foot Locker were worth zero, the whole $12.0B equity would be paying for the DICK'S Business alone (the former Foot Locker holders own part of the core too, so one cannot divide by the 79.3M legacy shares). On FY26 guide midpoints, DICK'S segment profit is about $1.57B, so after roughly $0.05-0.1B of other costs and interest, and a 29% tax rate (the guide's rate includes Foot Locker foreign losses; a lower rate is arguable), core earnings are roughly $1.1B, which is about 10-11x the market cap (my approximation, not company disclosure). The shares issued to Foot Locker holders are worth $1.3B today versus $2.1B at the deal. This only works if the DICK'S segment margin holds near 10.5% (see H1) and Foot Locker stops burning cash.

**Why it matters.** It frames the long case as optionality (Foot Locker at breakeven is worth well above zero; DICK'S pre-deal EPS growth was in the high single digits) and frames downside as Foot Locker cash burn relative to market cap. As an upper bound, $0.4B of capex plus the ~$0.06B segment loss is ~$0.45B a year (about 4% of market cap); the true burn is lower because the segment loss is already after depreciation, which I could not split out.

**Test datasets.** SEC XBRL segment data, quarterly capex split, FINRA short interest (squeeze potential: 8.0M shares short, 2.5 days to cover per equibles), insider purchases (Form 4), 13F changes after the cut.

**For.** Insider buying including the CFO and the Foot Locker International president; $3.0B buyback authorization; Baa2/BBB ratings; P/E ~11x on guide.

**Against.** $1.0B of new debt at 6.2-6.9% rather than buying back stock suggests cash is tight (FY25 free cash flow ~$0.4B vs dividends ~$0.4B and buybacks $0.35B; FY26 net capex of $1.4B); market may be right that the core growth is slowing (H1); Foot Locker lease liabilities are not in my EV; I have not independently built a SOTP.

### H4 (watch item): mix shift raises Nike exposure just as Nike's wholesale strategy shifts

**Claim.** Nike share of DKS purchases rose from ~25% (FY24, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089063/000108906325000012/dks-20250201.htm) to ~31% (FY25, 10-K) with only ~5 months of Foot Locker; a full year of Foot Locker likely pushes it higher [UNVERIFIED projection]. Footwear is now 40% of sales. If Nike's re-engagement with wholesale (NA wholesale +9%, Nike Direct NA -6% in Nike's Aug-quarter) is also a push of classic franchises into a promotional channel, DKS and Foot Locker take the markdown risk. The BLS footwear CPI offers a contrary read: footwear CPI was +3.4% to +5.2% YoY in May-Aug 2026 (bls_cpi_footwear_apparel.csv), which says the problem is product-cycle and mix at Foot Locker, not industry-wide price deflation.

**Why it matters.** It separates "industry promotion" (cyclical, likely to ease) from "Foot Locker legacy product" (structural to the assortment), which determines whether FY27 EPS can rebound toward $14.

**Test datasets.** Nike 10-Q/10-K, BLS CPI, Foot Locker/Champs assortment scrape (Salomon, On, New Balance share), Google Trends.

**For.** CPI above; management's own words on "legacy silhouettes" and success of Nike Mind and adidas; Nike restructuring and Sportswear/Jordan underperformance (Nike call summary). **Against.** CPI is quality-adjusted and may not capture promotional depth in athletic; I did not find a primary-source breakdown of Foot Locker's Nike share post-deal.

**Crowdedness note.** The Foot Locker bear case is now crowded: the stock fell about 31% in one day, there are multiple class-action notices, and short interest (8.0M shares) is near its six-month high. The less crowded angles are the DICK'S core two-year-stack test (H1) and the Foot Locker inventory derivation (H2), both of which rest on company-disclosed numbers.
